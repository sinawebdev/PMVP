# Ghana payroll deductions, pensions, reliefs and overtime

Checked **20 October 2026** against GRA, SSNIT and NPRA sources. These notes distinguish published rules from unresolved implementation choices. The September 2026 PAYE band change does not itself change these deductions.

## Pension facts and limits

- Act 766 section 3 and SSNIT's contribution FAQ give **employee 5.5%** and **employer 13%**, total **18.5%**. Remittances are **13.5% to SSNIT/Tier 1** and **5% to the Tier 2 trustee**. Employer contributions are an employer cost, not an additional deduction from employee net pay. [S1, S2]
- SSNIT's notice dated **13 January 2026**, effective **1 January 2026**, gives monthly minimum insurable earnings **GH¢587.80** and maximum **GH¢69,000.00**, with contributions **payable to SSNIT** of **GH¢79.40** and **GH¢9,315.00**. [S3]
- Act 766 section 63(2)–(3) states the Tier 1 minimum/maximum in terms of **13.5%**. The 2026 notice does not expressly extend this ceiling to Tier 2 or explain how to allocate a capped Tier 1 remittance between employee and employer amounts. [S1, S3]
- The generic SSNIT FAQ still contains an older **GH¢25,000** salary ceiling. The dated 2026 notice supersedes that figure; use the FAQ only for the contribution split. [S2, S3]

**Unresolved:** these sources do not settle whether a particular employer must cap the full employee 5.5%/employer 13% base, leave Tier 2 uncapped, or direct a separately specified excess contribution. Do not silently infer those allocations. Payroll above/below the published limits needs an explicitly verified pension treatment. Partial months, zero-pay months and an employee's exemption status also need case-specific evidence before applying a full-month minimum.

**Precision discrepancy:** 587.80 × 13.5% is 79.353, normally 79.35 to two decimals; the official notice prints **79.40**. Both published figures are retained here. Do not alter one silently to make them reconcile.

The NPRA-hosted Basic National Social Security Regulations file says in regulation 11(5) that the employer covers a shortfall against the minimum and in 11(6) that SSNIT sets the salary ceiling. However, that copy has a blank L.I. number and making date on PDF page 4. Its identity as the enacted edition was **not established**. It is saved as supporting material, not used to resolve the uncertainties above. [S4]

## Voluntary provident fund

The PAYE page describes a tax deduction limited to **16.5% of basic salary**, contributed by the employer, employee, or both. Act 766 section 112(2) and NPRA guideline 13.7.2 make this a **combined** limit, with deduction to the extent of each party's respective contributions; their wording is “monthly income.” For ordinary payroll the narrower basic-salary basis follows GRA's operational page. [S1, S5, S6]

Scheme registration and approved/licensed trustees must be established; an arbitrary savings or deduction item named “PF” is not evidence of a qualifying provident scheme. [S5]

The employee's qualifying contribution reduces employee chargeable income. The employer's own contribution is not a second employee cash deduction. GRA's employment assessment format includes **excess employer retirement contributions** as taxable employment benefits. [S6, S7]

**Unresolved:** the sources do not state whether employee or employer contributions consume the shared cap first when their combined contributions exceed it. An allocation rule must be recorded and supported by scheme/GRA guidance; “employer first” or “employee first” must not be described as a verified statutory rule. Do not grant both parties a separate 16.5% allowance.

## Personal reliefs

GRA describes these as reliefs for **resident individuals**, following an application to the Commissioner-General. [S8]

| Relief | Published annual amount | Eligibility/limit on GRA page |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Marriage/responsibility | GH¢1,200 | Supports spouse or at least two children |
| Child education | GH¢600 per child | Maximum 3; recognised registered institution in Ghana; adopted child/ward included; one claimant per child |
| Disability | 25% of employment/business income | Disability established to Commissioner's satisfaction; investment income excluded |
| Old age | GH¢1,500 | Age 60 or over |
| Aged dependent relative | GH¢1,000 per relative | Maximum 2; relative age 60+; spouse/child excluded; one claimant per relative |
| Training/education | GH¢2,000 | Training updates professional, technical or vocational skills/knowledge; record approved amount |

The relief application form's official-use section records the **amount granted**, the number of months used for the monthly deduction and the **first deductible month**, then transfer to the tax relief card. Apply the approved amount only within its approved period/year. Do not automatically divide by twelve or backdate a relief merely because a profile is marked married, disabled or age 60+. [S9]

Exact historical commencement dates of the current relief amounts were not independently established from an accessible primary amendment in this research. The table records the current GRA page as checked on 20 October 2026; it does not claim these are new September 2026 reliefs.

## Overtime

The special resident overtime rates require **both** junior-staff status and **annual qualifying employment income not exceeding GH¢18,000**. GRA's current page refers to the employee's gains/profits to be taxed in the year, not only basic salary. Do not infer annual qualification solely from a low current monthly basic salary. [S6, S7]

For a qualifying employee, overtime up to **50% of monthly basic salary** is taxed at **5%**, and the excess at **10%**. The practice note's printed pages 15–16 provide examples: junior staff above the qualification threshold and senior staff receive no special overtime rate; their entire overtime joins ordinary taxable employment income. The current PAYE page separately states **20%** for non-resident bonus/overtime. [S6, S7]

Record junior status and the verified annual qualifying-income amount or basis. Missing eligibility evidence must not produce an automatic concession. The tax threshold does not itself determine whether employment law permits or requires overtime pay.
